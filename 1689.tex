\chapter{Of the Holy Scriptures}

\paragraph{1}
The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible \R{}rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience,\bi{} 
although the light \R{}of nature and the works of creation and providence do so far manifest the goodness, wisdom, and power of God, as to leave men inexcusable;\bi{}
yet \renihan{are they}{\note{they}{those means} are} not sufficient to give that knowledge of God and his will which is necessary unto salvation. 

\R{}Therefore it pleased the Lord at sundry times and in divers manners to reveal himself\, and to declare that his will unto his church; 

and afterward,
\begin{itemize}
\item for the better preserving and propagating of the truth,
\item and for the more sure establishment and comfort of the church against the corruption of the flesh,
\item and the malice of Satan and of the world,
\end{itemize}
to commit the same wholly unto \R{}writing; which maketh the Holy Scriptures to be most necessary,\lb
those former ways of God’s revealing his will unto his people being now ceased.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{2 Ti 3:15-17; Isa 8:20; Luk 16:29,31; Eph 2:20}
\C{2}{Rom 1:19-21; 2:14-15; Psa 19:1-3}
\C{3}{Heb 1:1}
\C{4}{Pro 22:19-21; Rom 15:4; 2 Pe 1:19-20}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
Under the name of Holy Scripture, or the Word of God written, are now contained all the books of the Old and New Testaments, which are these:

\subparagraph{Of the Old Testament}
Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 \& 2 Samuel, 1 \& 2 Kings, 1 \& 2 Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther, Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel, Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi.

\subparagraph{Of the New Testament}
Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Acts, Romans, 1 \& 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 \& 2 Thessalonians, 1 \& 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon, Hebrews, James, 1 \& 2 Peter, 1, 2, \& 3 John, Jude, Revelation.

All of which are given by the \R{}inspiration of God to be the rule of faith and life.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{5}{2 Ti 3:16}
\end{enumerate}
      
\paragraph{3} 
The books commonly called Apocrypha, not being of \R{}divine inspiration, are \modernize{no}{not} part of the canon or rule of the Scripture, and, therefore, are of no authority to the church of God, nor to be any otherwise approved or made use of than other human writings.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{6}{Luk 24:27,44; Rom 3:2}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
\informal
{
The authority of the Holy Scripture, for which it ought to be believed, \modernize{dependeth not}{does not depend} upon the testimony of any man or church, but wholly upon \R{}God (who is truth itself), the author thereof;\bi{} therefore it is to be received because it is the Word of God.
} {
The authority of the Holy Scripture does not depend upon the testimony of any man or Church, but wholly upon \R{}God who is truth itself. The Holy Scripture ought to be belived and received because it is authored by God and is thus the Word of God.
}\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{7}{2 Pe 1:19-21; 2 Ti 3:16; 2 Th 2:13; 1 Jo 5:9}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
We may be moved and induced by the testimony of the church of God to \modernize{an}{a} high and reverent esteem of the Holy Scriptures;\lb{}

\ano{Additionally}
\begin{itemize}
\item \trevitz{and}{} the heavenliness of the matter, 
\item     the efficacy of the doctrine, 
\item \trevitz{and}{} the majesty of the style, 
\item     the consent of all the parts, 
\item     the scope of the whole (which is to give all glory to God), 
\item     the full discovery \note{it}{the Holy Scriptures} makes of the only way of man’s salvation, 
\item \trevitz{and}{} many other incomparable excellencies, and entire perfections thereof, 
\end{itemize}
are arguments whereby \note{it}{the Holy Scriptures} \modernize{doth}{does} abundantly evidence itself to be the Word of God; 

yet notwithstanding our \R{}full persuasion and assurance of the infallible truth\, and \trevitz{}{the} divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit bearing witness by and with the Word in our hearts.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{8}{Joh 16:13-14; 1 Co 2:10-12; 1 Jo 2:20,27}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
The whole counsel of God concerning all things \R{}necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down or necessarily contained in the Holy Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelation of the Spirit, or traditions of men.\bi{} Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inward \R{}illumination of the Spirit of God to be necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the Word, and that there are some circumstances concerning \ano{both} the worship of God\, and government of the church, common to human actions and societies, which are to be \R{}ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word, which are always to be observed.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{9}{2 Ti 3:15-17; Gal 1:8-9}
\C{10}{Joh 6:45; 1Co 2:9-12}
\C{11}{1 Co 11:13-14; 14:26,40}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{7}
\informal
{
All things in Scripture are not alike \R{}plain in themselves, nor alike clear unto all; yet those things which are necessary to be known, believed and observed for salvation, are so \R{}clearly propounded and opened in some place of Scripture or other, that not only the learned, but the unlearned, in a due use of ordinary means, may attain to a sufficient understanding of them.
} {
The doctrines of the Holy Scriptures are not equally \R{}understable, and are not made equally clear to all; yet those things which are necessary to be known, believed, and observed for salvation, are so \R{}clearly propounded and opened within various parts of the Scripture, that all mankind by using ordinary means, regardless of their education, may attain to a sufficient understanding of them.
}\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{12}{2 Pe 3:16}
\C{13}{Psa 19:7; Psa 119:130}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{8}
The Old Testament in \R{}Hebrew (which was the native language of the people of God of old), and the New Testament in Greek (which at the time of the writing of it was most generally known to the nations), being immediately inspired by God, and by his singular care and providence kept pure in all ages, are therefore \R{}authentic; so as in all controversies of religion, the church is finally to appeal to them. \bi{}\R{}But because these original tongues are not known to all the people of God, who have a right unto, and interest in the Scriptures, and are commanded in the fear of God to read \R{}and search them, therefore they are to be translated into the \informal{vulgar}{native} language of every nation unto which they \R{}come, that \ano{with} the Word of God dwelling \R{}plentifully in all, they may worship him in an acceptable manner, and through patience and comfort of the Scriptures may have hope.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{14}{Rom 3:2}
\C{15}{Isa 8:20}
\C{16}{Act 15:15}
\C{17}{Joh 5:39}
\C{18}{1 Co 14:6,9,11-12,24,28}
\C{19}{Col 3:16}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{9}
The infallible \informal{rule of interpretation}{interpreter} of Scripture is the \R{}Scripture itself; and therefore when there is a question about the true and full sense of any Scripture (which is not manifold, but one), it must be searched by other places that speak more clearly.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{20}{2 Pe 1:20-21; Act 15:15-16}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{10}
The supreme judge,
\begin{itemize}
\item by which all controversies of religion are to be determined,
\item \trevitz{and}{by which} all decrees of councils, opinions of ancient writers, doctrines of men, and private spirits, are to be examined, 
\item \trevitz{and} in whose sentence we are to rest, 
\end{itemize}
can \modernize{be no other but}{only be} the Holy Scripture delivered by the Spirit, into which \R{}Scripture so delivered, our faith is finally resolved.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{21}{Mat 22:29,31-32; Eph 2:20; Act 28:23}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of God and Of the Holy Trinity}

\paragraph{1}
The Lord our God is
\begin{itemize}
\item but \R{}one only living and true God; whose \R{}subsistence is in and of himself, 
\item \R{}infinite in being and perfection; whose essence cannot be comprehended by any but himself; 
\item \R{}a most pure spirit, 
\item \R{}invisible, without body, parts, or passions, who only \modernize{hath}{has} immortality, % TODO: What does it mean that only God has immortality?
\item dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto; 
\end{itemize}
who is
\begin{itemize}
\item \R{}immutable, \R{}immense, \R{}eternal, incomprehensible, \R{}almighty, every way infinite,
\item \R{}most holy, most wise, most free, most absolute; 
\item \R{}working all things according to the counsel of his own immutable and most righteous will \R{}for his own glory; 
\item most loving, gracious, merciful, longsuffering, abundant in goodness and truth, 
\item forgiving iniquity, transgression, and sin; 
\item \R{}the rewarder of them that diligently seek him, 
\end{itemize}
and \modernize{\define{withal}{in addition is}}{} most just \R{}and terrible in his judgments, \R{}hating all sin, and who will by no means clear the \R{}guilty.\lb{}

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2} 
God, having all \R{}life, \R{}glory, \R{}goodness, blessedness, in and of himself, is alone in and unto himself all-sufficient, not \R{}standing in need of any creature which he hath made, nor deriving any glory from them, but only manifesting his own glory in, by, unto, and upon them;\lb{}
\begin{itemize}
\item he is the alone fountain of all being, \R{}of whom, through whom, and to whom are all things,
\item \trevitz{and}{} he hath most sovereign \R{}dominion over all creatures, to do by them, for them, or upon them, whatsoever himself pleaseth;
\item in his sight \R{}all things are open and manifest;
\item his knowledge is \R{}infinite, infallible, and independent upon the creature, so as nothing is to him contingent or uncertain;
\item he is most holy in all his counsels, in \R{}all his works, and in all his commands;
\item to him is due \R{}from angels and men, whatsoever worship, service, or obedience, as creatures they owe unto the Creator, and whatever he is further pleased to require of them.\lb{}
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\C{24}{Eze 11:5; Act 15:18}
\C{25}{Psa 145:17}
\C{26}{Rev 5:12-14}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
In this divine and infinite Being there are three subsistences, \R{}the Father, the Word or Son, and Holy Spirit, of one substance, power, and eternity, each having the whole divine essence, \R{}yet the essence undivided: 
\begin{enumerate}
\item the Father is of none, neither begotten nor proceeding; 
\item the Son is \R{}eternally begotten of the Father; 
\item the Holy Spirit \R{}proceeding from the Father and the Son; 
\end{enumerate}
all infinite, without beginning, therefore but one God, who is not to be divided in nature and being, but distinguished by several peculiar relative properties and personal relations; which doctrine of the Trinity is the foundation of all our communion with God, and comfortable dependence on him.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\chapter{Of God’s Decree}

\paragraph{1}
God hath \R{}decreed in himself,
\begin{itemize}
\item from all eternity, 
\item by the most wise and holy counsel of his own will, 
\item freely and unchangeably, 
\end{itemize}
all things, whatsoever comes to pass;\lb{}

yet so as \modernize{thereby is God}{God is} neither 
\begin{itemize}
\item the author of sin 
\item \R{}nor hath fellowship with any therein; 
\item nor is violence offered to the will of the creature, \
\item nor yet is the liberty or contingency of \define{second causes}{TODO: Define second causes} taken away, but rather \R{}established\trevitz{;}{.}\lb{}
\end{itemize}

\trevitz{in which appears}{Within the second causes we can see} his wisdom in disposing all things, and \trevitz{}{the} power and faithfulness \R{}in accomplishing his decree.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Isa 46:10; Eph 1:11; Heb 6:17; Rom 9:15,18}
\C{2}{Jam 1:13; 1Jo 1:5}
\C{3}{Act 4:27-28; Joh 19:11}
\C{4}{Num 23:19; Eph 1:3-5}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
\trevitz{

} {

}\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{5}{Act 15:18}
\C{6}{Rom 9:11,13,16,18}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
By the decree of God, for the manifestation of his glory, \R{}some men and angels are predestinated or foreordained to eternal life through Jesus Christ, to the \R{}praise of his glorious grace;\bi{} \informal{others being}{the rest are} left to act in their sin to their \R{}just condemnation, to the praise of his glorious justice.\lb{}

\C{7}{1 Ti 5:21; Mat 25:34}
\C{8}{Eph 1:5-6}
\C{9}{Rom 9:22-23; Jude 4}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
These angels and men thus predestinated and foreordained, are particularly and unchangeably designed, and their \R{}number so certain and definite, that it cannot be either increased or diminished.\lb{}
  
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{10}{2Ti 2:19; Joh 13:18}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
Those of mankind \R{}that are predestinated to life, God, \bi{}\indent{}
before the foundation of the world was laid, \bi{}\indent{}
according to his eternal and immutable purpose, \bi{}\indent{}
and the secret counsel and good pleasure of his will, \bi{}
hath chosen in Christ unto everlasting glory, \bi{}\indent{}
out of his mere free grace and love, \bi{}\indent{}
\R{}without any other thing in the creature as a condition or cause moving him thereunto.\lb{}

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
As God hath appointed the elect unto glory, so he hath, by the eternal and most free purpose of his will, foreordained \R{}all the means thereunto; wherefore they who are elected, being fallen in Adam,
\begin{itemize}
\item \R{}are redeemed by Christ,
\item are effectually \R{}called unto faith in Christ, by his Spirit working in due season,
\item are justified, adopted, sanctified, and kept by his power through faith \R{}unto salvation;
\item neither are any other redeemed by Christ, or effectually called, justified, adopted, sanctified, and saved, but the elect \R{}only.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\paragraph{7}
The doctrine of this high mystery of predestination is to be handled with special prudence and care, that men attending the will of God revealed in his Word, and yielding obedience thereunto, may, from the certainty of their effectual vocation, be assured of their \R{}eternal election; so shall this doctrine afford matter \R{}of praise, reverence, and admiration of God, and \R{}of humility, diligence, and \R{}abundant consolation to all that sincerely obey the gospel.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of Divine Providence}

\paragraph{1}
God\modernize{}{,} the good Creator of all things,\bi{}in his infinite power and wisdom doth \R{}uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures and things,
\begin{itemize}
\item from the greatest even to the \R{}least, 
\item by his most wise and holy providence,
\item to the end for \modernize{the which}{which} they were created,
\item according \modernize{unto}{to} his infallible foreknowledge,
\item \trevitz{and}{according to} the free and immutable counsel of his \R{}own will;
\item to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness, and mercy.\lb{}
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Heb 1:3; Job 38:11; Isa 46:10-11; Psa 135:6}
\C{2}{Mat 10:29-31}
\C{3}{Eph 1:11}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
Although in relation to the foreknowledge and decree of God, the \define{first cause}{God's Decree is the first cause of all substance and activity}, all things come to pass \R{}immutably and infallibly; so that there is \trevitz{not anything befalls any}{nothing that happens} \R{}by chance, or without his providence; yet by the same providence he \modernize{ordereth}{orders} them to fall out according to the nature of second causes, either \R{}necessarily, freely, or contingently.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{4}{Act 2:23}
\C{5}{Pro 16:33}
\C{6}{Gen 8:22}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
God, in his ordinary providence \R{}\modernize{maketh}{makes} use of means, yet is free \R{}to work without, \R{}above, and \R{}against them at his pleasure.\lb{}


\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{7}{Act 27:31,44; Isa 55:10-11}
\C{8}{Hos 1:7}
\C{9}{Rom 4:19-21}
\C{10}{Dan 3:27}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
The almighty power, unsearchable wisdom, and infinite goodness of God, so far manifest themselves in his providence\trevitz{,}{} that his determinate counsel \R{}\modernize{extendeth}{extends} itself even to the first fall, and all other sinful actions both of angels and men;\bi{} and that not by a bare permission, which also he most wisely and powerfully \R{}\modernize{boundeth}{bounds}, and otherwise \modernize{ordereth and governeth}{orders and governs}, in a manifold \define{dispensation}{act of divine providence} to his most holy \R{}ends;\bi{} yet so, as the sinfulness of their acts \modernize{proceedeth}{proceeds} only from the creatures, and not from God, who, being most holy and righteous, neither is nor can be the author or \R{}approver of sin.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{11}{Rom 11:32-34; 2 Sa 24:1; 1Ch 21:1}
\C{12}{2 Ki 19:28; Psa 76:10}
\C{13}{Gen 50:20; Isa 10:6-7,12}
\C{14}{Psa 50:21; 1 Jo 2:16}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
The most wise, righteous, and gracious God doth oftentimes leave for a season his own children to manifold temptations and the corruptions of their own hearts, to chastise them for their former sins, or 
\begin{itemize}
\item to discover unto them the hidden strength of corruption and deceitfulness of their hearts, \R{}that they may be humbled; 
\item \trevitz{and}{} to raise them to a more close and constant dependence for their support upon himself;
\item \trevitz{and}{} to make them more watchful against all future occasions of sin, 
\item \trevitz{and}{} for other just and holy ends. 
\end{itemize}

So that whatsoever befalls any of his elect is by his appointment, for his glory, \R{}and their good.\lb{}


\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{15}{2 Ch 32:25-26,31; 2 Co 12:7-9}
\C{16}{Rom 8:28}

\chapter{Of Justification}

\paragraph{1}
Those whom God effectually calleth, he also freely \R{}justifieth, not by infusing righteousness into them, but by \R{}pardoning their sins, and by accounting and accepting their persons as \R{}righteous; not for anything wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ’s sake alone; not by imputing faith itself, the act of believing, or any other \R{}evangelical obedience to them, as their righteousness; but by imputing Christ’s active obedience unto the whole law, and passive obedience in his death for their whole and sole righteousness, they \R{}receiving and resting on him and his righteousness, by faith, which faith they have not of themselves; it is the gift of God.\lb{}

\C{6}{Rom 3:28}

By this faith a Christian believeth to be true \Rs{}whatsoever is revealed in the Word for the authority of God himself, and also apprehendeth an excellency therein \R{}above all other writings and all things in the world, as it bears forth the glory of God in his attributes, the excellency of Christ in his nature and offices, and the power and fullness of the Holy Spirit in his workings and operations: and so is enabled to \R{}cast his soul upon the truth thus believed; and also acteth differently upon that which each particular passage thereof containeth; yielding obedience to the \R{}commands, trembling at the \R{}threatenings, and embracing the \R{}promises of God for this life and that which is to come; but the principal acts of saving faith have immediate relation to Christ, accepting, receiving, and resting upon \R{}him alone for justification, sanctification, and eternal life, by virtue of the covenant of grace.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\paragraph{3}
This faith, although it be different in degrees, and may be weak \R{}or strong, yet it is in the least degree of it different in the kind or nature of it, as is all other saving grace, from the faith \R{}and common grace of temporary believers; and therefore, though it may be many times assailed and weakened, yet it gets \R{}the victory, growing up in many to the attainment of a full \R{}assurance through Christ, who is both the author \R{}and finisher of our faith.\lb{}

\C{1}{Ti 3:2-5}

\C{2}{Ecc 7:20}
\C{3}{Luk 22:31-32}
\end{enumerate}

\C{6}{Psa 119:6,128}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
As repentance is to be continued through the whole course of our lives, upon the account of the body of death, and the motions thereof, so it is every man’s duty to repent of his \R{}particular known sins particularly.\lb{}

\C{7}{Luk 19:8; 1 Ti 1:13,15}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
Such is the provision which God hath made through Christ in the covenant of grace for the preservation of believers unto salvation; that although there is no sin so small but it deserves \R{}damnation; yet there is no sin so great that it shall bring damnation on them that \R{}repent; which makes the constant preaching of repentance necessary.\lb{}

\chapter{Of Good Works}

\paragraph{1}
Good works are only such as God hath \R{}commanded in his Holy Word, and not such as without the warrant thereof are devised by men out of blind zeal, \R{}or upon any pretence of good intentions.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Mic 6:8; Heb 13:21}
\C{2}{Mat 15:9; Isa 29:13}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
These good works, done in obedience to God’s commandments, are the fruits and evidences \R{}of a true and lively faith; and by them believers manifest their \R{}thankfulness, strengthen their \R{}assurance, edify their \R{}brethren, adorn the profession of the gospel, stop the mouths of the adversaries, and glorify \R{}God, whose workmanship they are, created in Christ Jesus \R{}thereunto, that having their fruit unto holiness they may have the end \R{}eternal life.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{3}{Jam 2:18,22}
\C{4}{Psa 116:12-13}
\C{5}{1 Jo 2:3,5; 2 Pe 1:5-11}
\C{6}{Mat 5:16}
\C{7}{1 Ti 6:1; 1 Pe 2:15; Phi 1:11}
\C{8}{Eph 2:10}
\C{9}{Rom 6:22}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
Their ability to do good works is not at all of themselves, but wholly from the Spirit \R{}of Christ; and that they may be enabled thereunto, besides the graces they have already received, there is necessary and \R{}actual influence of the same Holy Spirit, to work in them to will and to do of his good pleasure; yet they are not hereupon to grow negligent, as if they were not bound to perform any duty, unless upon a special motion of the Spirit, but they ought to be diligent in \R{}stirring up the grace of God that is in them.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{10}{Joh 15:4-5}
\C{11}{2 Co 3:5; Phi 2:13}
\C{12}{Phi 2:12; Heb 6:11-12; Isa 64:7}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
They who in their obedience attain to the greatest height which is possible in this life, are so far from being able to supererogate, and to do more than God requires, as that \R{}they fall short of much which in duty they are bound to do.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{13}{Job 9:2-3; Gal 5:17; Luk 17:10}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
We cannot by our best works merit pardon of sin or eternal life at the hand of God, by reason of the great disproportion that is between them and the glory to come, and the infinite distance that is between us and God, whom by them we can neither profit nor satisfy for the debt of our \R{}former sins; but when we have done all we can, we have done but our duty, and are unprofitable servants; and because as they are good they proceed from his \R{}Spirit, and as they are wrought by us they are defiled \R{}and mixed with so much weakness and imperfection, that they cannot endure the severity of God’s punishment.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{14}{Rom 3:20; Eph 2:8-9; Rom 4:6}
\C{15}{Gal 5:22-23}
\C{16}{Isa 64:6; Psa 143:2}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
Yet notwithstanding the persons of believers being accepted through Christ, their good works also are accepted in \R{}him; not as though they were in this life wholly unblameable and unreprovable in God’s sight, but that he, looking upon them in his Son, is pleased to accept and reward that which is \R{}sincere, although accompanied with many weaknesses and imperfections.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{17}{Eph 1:6; 1Pe 2:5}
\C{18}{Mat 25:21,23; Heb 6:10}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{7}
Works done by unregenerate men, although for the matter of them they may be things which God commands, and of good use both to themselves and \R{}others; yet because they proceed not from a heart purified by \R{}faith, nor are done in a right manner according to the \R{}Word, nor to a right end, the \R{}glory of God, they are therefore sinful, and cannot please God, nor make a man meet to receive grace from \R{}God, and yet their neglect of them is more sinful and \R{}displeasing to God.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]

\chapter{Of the Assurance of Grace and Salvation}

\paragraph{1}
Although temporary believers, and other unregenerate men, may vainly deceive themselves with false hopes and carnal presumptions of being in the favour of God and state of salvation, \R{}which hope of theirs shall perish; yet such as truly believe in the Lord Jesus, and love him in sincerity, endeavouring to walk in all good conscience before him, may in this life be certainly assured \R{}that they are in the state of grace, and may rejoice in the hope of the glory of God, which hope shall never make them \R{}ashamed.\lb{}

\C{1}{Job 8:13-14; Mat 7:22-23}
\C{2}{1 Jo 2:3; 3:14,18-19,21,24; 5:13}
\C{3}{Rom 5:2,5}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
This certainty is not a bare conjectural and probable persuasion grounded upon \R{}a fallible hope, but an infallible assurance of faith founded on the blood and righteousness of Christ \R{}revealed in the Gospel; and also upon the inward \R{}evidence of those graces of the Spirit unto which promises are made, and on the testimony of the \R{}Spirit of adoption, witnessing with our spirits that we are the children of God; and, as a fruit thereof, keeping the heart both \R{}humble and holy.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{4}{Heb 6:11,19}

\paragraph{3}
This infallible assurance doth not so belong to the essence of faith, but that a true believer may wait long, and conflict with many difficulties before he be \R{}partaker of it; yet being enabled by the Spirit to know the things which are freely given him of God, he may, without extraordinary revelation, in the right use of means, \R{}attain thereunto: and therefore it is the duty of every one to give all diligence to make his calling and election sure, that thereby his heart may be enlarged in peace and joy in the Holy Spirit, in love and thankfulness to God, and in strength and cheerfulness in the duties of obedience, the proper \R{}fruits of this assurance; so far is it \R{}from inclining men to looseness.\lb{}

\chapter{Of the Law of God}

\paragraph{1}
God gave to Adam a law of universal obedience \R{}written in his heart, and a particular precept of not eating the fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him and all his posterity to personal, entire, exact, and perpetual \R{}obedience; promised life upon the fulfilling, and \R{}threatened death upon the breach of it, and endued him with power and ability to keep it.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Gen 1:27; Ecc 7:29}
\C{2}{Rom 10:5}
\C{3}{Gal 3:10,12}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
The same law that was first written in the heart of man \R{}continued to be a perfect rule of righteousness after the fall, and was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in \R{}ten commandments, and written in two tables, the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{4}{Rom 2:14-15}
\C{5}{Deu 10:4}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
Besides this law, commonly called moral, God was pleased to give to the people of Israel ceremonial laws, containing several typical ordinances, partly of worship, \R{}prefiguring Christ, his graces, actions, sufferings, and benefits; and partly holding forth divers instructions \R{}of moral duties, all which ceremonial laws being appointed only to the time of reformation, are, by Jesus Christ the true Messiah and only law-giver, who was furnished with power from the Father for that end, \R{}abrogated and taken away.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{6}{Heb 10:1; Col 2:17}
\C{7}{1Co 5:7}
\C{8}{Col 2:14,16-17; Eph 2:14,16}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
To them also he gave sundry judicial laws, which expired together with the state of that people, not obliging any now by virtue of that institution; their general \R{}equity only being of moral use.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{9}{1 Co 9:8-10}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
The moral law doth for ever bind all, \R{}as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the \R{}authority of God the Creator, who gave it; neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, \R{}but much strengthen this obligation.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{10}{Rom 13:8-10; Jam 2:8,10-12}
\C{11}{Jam 2:10-11}
\C{12}{Mat 5:17-19; Rom 3:31}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
Although true believers be not under the law as a covenant of works, \R{}to be thereby justified or condemned, yet it is of great use to them as well as to others, in that as a rule of life, informing them of the will of God and their duty, it directs and binds them to walk accordingly; \R{}discovering also the sinful pollutions of their natures, hearts, and lives, so as examining themselves thereby, they may come to further conviction of, humiliation for, and hatred against, sin; together with a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ and the perfection of his obedience; it is likewise of use to the regenerate to restrain their corruptions, in that it forbids sin; and the threatenings of it serve to shew what even their sins deserve, and what afflictions in this life they may expect for them, although freed from the curse and unallayed rigour thereof. The promises of it likewise shew them God’s approbation of obedience, and what blessings they may expect upon the performance thereof, though not as due to them by the law as a covenant of works; so as man’s doing good and refraining from evil, because the law encourageth to the one and deterreth from the other, is no evidence of his being \R{}under the law and not under grace.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{13}{Rom 6:14; Gal 2:16; Rom 8:1; 10:4}
\C{14}{Rom 3:20; 7:7; etc.}
\C{15}{Rom 6:12-14; 1Pe 3:8-13}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{7}
Neither are the aforementioned uses of the law \R{}contrary to the grace of the Gospel, but do sweetly comply with it, the Spirit of Christ subduing \R{}and enabling the will of man to do that freely and cheerfully which the will of God, revealed in the law, requireth to be done.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{16}{Gal 3:21}
\C{17}{Eze 36:27}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of the Gospel, and Of the Extent of the Grace Thereof}

\paragraph{1}
The covenant of works being broken by sin, and made unprofitable unto life, God was pleased to give forth the promise of Christ, \R{}the seed of the woman, as the means of calling the elect, and begetting in them faith and repentance; in this promise the \R{}gospel, as to the substance of it, was revealed, and [is] therein effectual for the conversion and salvation of sinners.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Gen 3:15}
\C{2}{Rev 13:8}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
This promise of Christ, and salvation by him, is revealed only by \R{}the Word of God; neither do the works of creation or providence, with the light of nature, \R{}make discovery of Christ, or of grace by him, so much as in a general or obscure way; much less that men destitute of the revelation of Him by the promise or gospel, \R{}should be enabled thereby to attain saving faith or repentance.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{3}{Rom 1:17}
\C{4}{Rom 10:14-15,17}
\C{5}{Pro 29:18; Isa 25:7; 60:2-3}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
The revelation of the gospel unto sinners, made in divers times and by sundry parts, with the addition of promises and precepts for the obedience required therein, as to the nations and persons to whom it is granted, is merely of the \R{}sovereign will and good pleasure of God; not being annexed by virtue of any promise to the due improvement of men’s natural abilities, by virtue of common light received without it, which none ever did \R{}make, or can do so; and therefore in all ages, the preaching of the gospel has been granted unto persons and nations, as to the extent or straitening of it, in great variety, according to the counsel of the will of God.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{6}{Psa 147:20; Act 16:7}
\C{7}{Rom 1:18-32}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
Although the gospel be the only outward means of revealing Christ and saving grace, and is, as such, abundantly sufficient thereunto; yet that men who are dead in trespasses may be born again, quickened or regenerated, there is moreover necessary an effectual insuperable \R{}work of the Holy Spirit upon the whole soul, for the producing in them a new spiritual life; without which no other means will effect \R{}their conversion unto God.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{8}{Psa 110:3; 1Co 2:14; Eph 1:19-20}
\C{9}{Joh 6:44; 2Co 4:4,6}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of Christian Liberty and Liberty of Conscience}

\paragraph{1}
The liberty which Christ hath purchased for believers under the gospel, consists in their freedom from the guilt of sin, the condemning wrath of God, the rigour and \R{}curse of the law, and in their being delivered from this present evil \R{}world, bondage to \R{}Satan, and dominion \R{}of sin, from the \R{}evil of afflictions, the fear and sting \R{}of death, the victory
of the grave, and \R{}everlasting damnation: as also in their \R{}free access to God, and their yielding obedience unto Him, not out of slavish fear, \R{}but a child-like love and willing mind. All which were common also to believers under the law \R{}for the substance of them; but under the New Testament the liberty of Christians is further enlarged, in their freedom from the yoke of a ceremonial law, to which the Jewish church was subjected, and in greater boldness of access to the throne of grace, and in fuller communications of the \R{}free Spirit of God, than believers under the law did ordinarily partake of.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Gal 3:13}
\C{2}{Gal 1:4}
\C{3}{Act 26:18}
\C{4}{Rom 8:3}
\C{5}{Rom 8:28}
\C{6}{1Co 15:54-57}
\C{7}{2Th 1:10}
\C{8}{Rom 8:15}
\C{9}{Luk 1:73-75; 1 Jo 4:18}
\C{10}{Gal 3:9,14}
\C{11}{Joh 7:38-39; Heb 10:19-21}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
God alone is \R{}Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men \R{}which are in any thing contrary to his Word, or not contained in it. So that to believe such doctrines, or obey such commands out of conscience, \R{}is to betray true liberty of conscience; and the requiring of an \R{}implicit faith, an absolute and blind obedience, is to destroy liberty of conscience and reason also.\lb{}

\paragraph{3}
They who upon pretence of Christian liberty do practice any sin, or cherish any sinful lust, as they do thereby pervert the main design of the grace of the gospel \R{}to their own destruction, so they wholly destroy \R{}the end of Christian liberty, which is, that being delivered out of the hands of all our enemies, we might serve the Lord without fear, in holiness and righteousness before him, all the days of our lives.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{16}{Rom 6:1-2}

\chapter{Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day}

\paragraph{1}
The light of nature shews that there is a God, who hath lordship and sovereignty over all; is just, good and doth good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served, with all the heart and all the soul, \R{}and with all the might. But the acceptable way of worshipping the true God, is \R{} instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imagination and devices of men, nor the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representations, or \R{}any other way not prescribed in the Holy Scriptures.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Jer 10:7; Mar 12:33}
\C{2}{Deu 12:32}
\C{3}{Exo 20:4-6}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
Religious worship is to be given to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and to him \R{}alone; not to angels, saints, or any other \R{}creatures; and since the fall, not without a \R{}mediator, nor in the mediation of any other but \R{}Christ alone.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{4}{Mat 4:9-10; Joh 4:23; Mat 28:19}
\C{5}{Rom 1:25; Col 2:18; Rev 19:10}
\C{6}{Joh 14:6}
\C{7}{1 Ti 2:5}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
Prayer, with thanksgiving, being one part of natural worship, is by God required of \R{}all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the \R{}name of the Son, by the help \R{}of the Spirit, according to \R{}his will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a \R{}known tongue.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{8}{Psa 95:1-7; 65:2}
\C{9}{Joh 14:13-14}
\C{10}{Rom 8:26}
\C{11}{1 Jo 5:14}
\C{12}{1 Co 14:16-17}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
Prayer is to be made for things lawful, and for all sorts of men living, \R{}or that shall live hereafter; but not \R{}for the dead, nor for those of whom it may be known that they have sinned \R{}the sin unto death.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{13}{1 Ti 2:1-2; 2 Sa 7:29}
\C{14}{2 Sa 12:21-23}
\C{15}{1 Jo 5:16}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
The \R{}reading of the Scriptures, preaching, and \R{}hearing the Word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing with grace in our hearts to \R{}the Lord; as also the administration \R{}of baptism, and \R{}the Lord’s supper, are all parts of religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear; moreover, solemn humiliation, \R{}with fastings, and thanksgivings, upon \R{}special occasions, ought to be used in an holy and religious manner.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{16}{1 Ti 4:13}
\C{17}{2 Ti 4:2; Luk 8:18}
\C{18}{Col 3:16; Eph 5:19}
\C{19}{Mat 28:19-20}
\C{20}{1 Co 11:26}
\C{21}{Est 4:16; Joel 2:12}
\C{22}{Exo 15:1-19, Psa 107}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
Neither prayer nor any other part of religious worship, is now, under the gospel, tied unto, or made more acceptable by any place in which it is \R{}performed, or towards which it is directed; but God is to be worshipped everywhere in spirit and in truth; as in \R{}private families \R{}daily, and \R{}in secret each one by himself; so more solemnly in the public assemblies, which are not carelessly nor wilfully to be \R{}neglected or forsaken, when God by his Word or providence calleth thereunto.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{23}{Joh 4:21; Mal 1:11; 1Ti 2:8}
\C{24}{Act 10:2}
\C{25}{Mat 6:11; Psa 55:17}
\C{26}{Mat 6:6}
\C{27}{Heb 10:25; Act 2:42}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{7}
As it is the law of nature, that in general a proportion of time, by God’s appointment, be set apart for the worship of God, so by his Word, in a positive moral, and perpetual commandment, binding all men, in all ages, he hath particularly appointed one day in seven for a \R{}sabbath to be kept holy unto him, which from the beginning of the world to the resurrection of Christ was the last day of the week, and from the resurrection of Christ was changed into the first day of the week, \R{}which is called the Lord’s Day: and is to be continued to the end of the world as the Christian Sabbath, the observation of the last day of the week being abolished.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{28}{Exo 20:8}
\C{29}{1 Co 16:1-2; Act 20:7; Rev 1:10}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{8}
The sabbath is then kept holy unto the Lord, when men, after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand, do not only observe an holy \R{}rest all day, from their own works, words and thoughts, about their worldly employment and recreations, but are also taken up the whole time in the public and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties \R{}of necessity and mercy.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{30}{Isa 58:13; Neh 13:15-22}
\C{31}{Mat 12:1-13}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of Lawful Oaths and Vows}

\paragraph{1}
A lawful oath is a part of religious worship, \R{}wherein the person swearing in truth, righteousness, and judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth, \R{}and to judge him according to the truth or falseness thereof.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Exo 20:7; Deu 10:20; Jer 4:2}
\C{2}{2Ch 6:22-23}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
The name of God only is that by which men ought to swear; and therein it is to be used, with all holy fear and reverence; therefore to swear vainly or rashly by that glorious and dreadful name, or to swear at all by any other thing, is sinful, and to be \R{}abhorred; yet as in matter of weight and moment, for confirmation of truth, \R{}and ending all strife, an oath is warranted by the Word of God; so a lawful oath being imposed \R{}by lawful authority in such matters, ought to be taken.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{3}{Mat 5:34,37; Jam 5:12}
\C{4}{Heb 6:16, 2 Co 1:23}
\C{5}{Neh 13:25}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
Whosoever taketh an oath warranted by the Word of God, ought duly to consider the weightiness of so solemn an act, and therein to avouch nothing but what he knoweth to be truth; for that by rash, false, and vain oaths, the \R{}Lord is provoked, and for them this land mourns.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{6}{Lev 19:12; Jer 23:10}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
An oath is to be taken in the plain and \R{}common sense of the words, without equivocation or mental reservation.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{7}{Psa 24:4}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
A vow, which is not to be made to any creature, but to God alone, \R{}is to be made and performed with all religious care and faithfulness; but popish monastical vows \R{}of perpetual single life, professed \R{}poverty, and regular obedience, are so far from being degrees of higher perfection, that they are superstitious \R{}and sinful snares, in which no Christian may entangle himself.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{8}{Psa 76:11; Gen 28:20-22}
\C{9}{1 Co 7:2,9}
\C{10}{Eph 4:28}
\C{11}{Mat 19:11}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of the Church}

\paragraph{1}
The catholic or universal church, which (with respect to the internal work of the Spirit and truth of grace) may be called invisible, consists of the whole \R{}number of the elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one, under Christ, the head thereof; and is the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Heb 12:23; Col 1:18; Eph 1:10,22-23; 5:23,27,32}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
All persons throughout the world, professing the faith of the gospel, and obedience unto God by Christ according unto it, not destroying their own profession by any errors everting the foundation, or unholiness of conversation, \R{}are and may be called visible saints; \R{}and of such ought all particular congregations to be constituted.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{2}{1Co 1:2; Act 11:26}
\C{3}{Rom 1:7; Eph 1:20-22}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
The purest churches under heaven are subject \R{}to mixture and error; and some have so degenerated as to become \R{}no churches of Christ, but synagogues of Satan; nevertheless Christ always hath had, and ever shall have a \R{}kingdom in this world, to the end thereof, of such as believe in him, and make profession of his name.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{4}{1 Co 5; Rev 2-3}
\C{5}{Rev 18:2; 2 Th 2:11-12}
\C{6}{Mat 16:18; Psa 72:17; 102:28; Rev 12:17}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
The Lord Jesus Christ is the Head of the church, in whom, by the appointment of the Father, \R{}all power for the calling, institution, order or government of the church, is invested in a supreme and sovereign manner; neither can the Pope of Rome in any sense be head thereof, but is \R{}that antichrist, that man of sin, and son of perdition, that exalteth himself in the church against Christ, and all that is called God; whom the Lord shall destroy with the brightness of his coming.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{7}{Col 1:18; Mat 28:18-20; Eph 4:11-12}
\C{8}{2 Th 2:2-9}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
In the execution of this power wherewith he is so intrusted, the Lord Jesus calleth out of the world unto himself, through the ministry of his Word, by his Spirit, \R{}those that are given unto him by his Father, that they may walk before him in all the \R{}ways of obedience, which he prescribeth to them in his Word. Those thus called, he commandeth to walk together in particular societies, or \R{}churches, for their mutual edification, and the due performance of that public worship, which he requireth of them in the world.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{9}{Joh 10:16}
\C{10}{Joh 12:32; Mat 28:20}
\C{11}{Mat 18:15-20}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
The members of these churches are \R{}saints by calling, visibly manifesting and evidencing (in and by their profession and walking) their obedience unto that call of Christ; and do willingly consent to walk together, according to the appointment of Christ; giving up themselves to the Lord, and one to another, by the will of God, \R{}in professed subjection to the ordinances of the Gospel.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{12}{Rom 1:7; 1 Co 1:2}
\C{13}{Act 2:41-42; 5:13-14; 2 Co 9:13}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{7}
To each of these churches thus gathered, according to his mind declared in his Word, he hath given all that \R{}power and authority, which is in any way needful for their carrying on that order in worship and discipline, which he hath instituted for them to observe; with commands and rules for the due and right exerting, and executing of that power.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{14}{Mat 18:17-18; 1 Co 5:4-5; 5:13; 2 Co 2:6-8}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{8}
A particular church, gathered and completely organized according to the mind of Christ, consists of officers and members; and the officers appointed by Christ to be chosen and set apart by the church (so called and gathered), for the peculiar administration of ordinances, and execution of power or duty, which he intrusts them with, or calls them to, to be continued to the end of the world, are \R{}bishops or elders, and deacons.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{15}{Act 20:17,28; Phi 1:1}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{9}
The way appointed by Christ for the calling of any person, fitted and gifted by the Holy Spirit, unto the office of bishop or elder in a church, is, that he be chosen thereuntoby the common \R{}suffrage of the church itself; and solemnly set apart by  fasting and prayer, with imposition of hands of the \R{}eldership of the church, if there be any before constituted therein; and of a deacon \R{}that he be chosen by the like suffrage, and set apart by prayer, and the like imposition of hands.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{16}{Act 14:23}
\C{17}{1 Ti 4:14}
\C{18}{Act 6:3,5-6}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{10}
The work of pastors being constantly to attend the service of Christ, in his churches, in the ministry of the Word and prayer, \R{}with watching for their souls, as they that must give an account to him; it is incumbent on the churches to whom they minister, not only to give them all due respect, \R{}but also to communicate to them of all their good things according to their ability, so as they may have a comfortable supply, without being themselves \R{}entangled in secular affairs; and may also be capable of exercising \R{}hospitality towards others; and this is required by the \R{}law of nature, and by the express order of our Lord Jesus, who hath ordained that they that preach the gospel should live of the gospel.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{19}{Act 6:4; Heb 13:17}
\C{20}{1 Ti 5:17-18; Gal 6:6-7}
\C{21}{2 Ti 2:4}
\C{22}{1 Ti 3:2} 
\C{23}{1 Co 9:6-14}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{11}
Although it be incumbent on the bishops or pastors of the churches, to be instant in
preaching the Word, by way of office, yet the work of preaching the Word is not so peculiarly confined to them but that others also \R{}gifted and fitted by the Holy Spirit for it, and approved and called by the church, may and ought to perform it.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{24}{Act 11:19-21; 1 Pe 4:10-11}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{12}
As all believers are bound to join themselves to particular churches, when and where they have opportunity so to do; so all that are admitted unto the privileges of a church, are also \R{}under the censures and government thereof, according to the rule of Christ.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{25}{1 Th 5:14; 2 Th 3:6,14-15}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{13}
No church members, upon any offence taken by them, having performed their duty
required of them towards the person they are offended at, ought to disturb any churchorder, or absent themselves from the assemblies of the church, or administration of any
ordinances, upon the account of such offence at any of their fellow members, but to wait
upon Christ, \R{}in the further proceeding of the church.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{26}{Mat 18:15-17; Eph 4:2-3}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{14}
As each church, and all the members of it, are bound to \R{}pray continually for the good and prosperity of all the churches of Christ, in all places, and upon all occasions to further every one within the bounds of their places and callings, in the exercise of their gifts and graces, so the churches, when planted by the providence of God, so as they may enjoy opportunity and advantage for it, ought to hold \R{}communion among themselves, for their peace, increase of love, and mutual edification.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{27}{Eph 6:18; Psa 122:6}
\C{28}{Rom 16:1-2; 3 Jo 8-10}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{15}
In cases of difficulties or differences, either in point of doctrine or administration, wherein either the churches in general are concerned, or any one church, in their peace, union, and edification; or any member or members of any church are injured, in or by any proceedings in censures not agreeable to truth and order: it is according to the mind of Christ, that many churches holding communion together, do, by their messengers, meet to consider, \R{}and give their advice in or about that matter in difference, to be reported to all the churches concerned; howbeit these messengers assembled, are not intrusted with any church-power properly so called; or with any jurisdiction over the churches themselves, to exercise any censures either over any churches or persons; or \R{}to impose their determination on the churches or officers.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{29}{Act 15:2,4,6,22-23,25}
\C{30}{2 Co 1:24; 1 Jo 4:1}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of the Lord’s Supper}

\paragraph{1}
The supper of the Lord Jesus was instituted by him the same night wherein he was betrayed, to be observed in his churches, unto the end of the world, for the perpetual remembrance, and shewing forth the sacrifice of himself in his death, \R{}confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof, their spiritual nourishment, and growth in him, their further engagement in and to all duties which they owe to him; \R{}and to be a bond and pledge of their communion with him, and with each other.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{1 Co 11:23-26}
\C{2}{1 Co 10:16-17,21}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
In this ordinance Christ is not offered up to his Father, nor any real sacrifice made at all for remission of sin of the quick or dead, but only a memorial of that \R{}one offering up of himself by himself upon the cross, once for all; and a spiritual oblation of all \R{}possible praise unto God for the same. So that the popish sacrifice of the mass, as they call it, is most abominable, injurious to Christ’s own sacrifice the alone propitiation for all the sins of the elect.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{3}{Heb 9:25-26,28}
\C{4}{1 Co 11:24; Mat 26:26-27}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}
The Lord Jesus hath, in this ordinance, appointed his ministers to pray, and bless the elements of bread and wine, and thereby to set them apart from a common to a holy use, and to take and break the bread; to take the cup, \R{}and, they communicating also themselves, to give both to the communicants.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{5}{1 Co 11:23-26, etc.}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4}
The denial of the cup to the people, worshipping the elements, the lifting them up, or carrying them about for adoration, and reserving them for any pretended religious use, \R{}are all contrary to the nature of this ordinance, and to the institution of Christ.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{6}{Mat 26:26-28; 15:9; Exo 20:4-5}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{5}
The outward elements in this ordinance, duly set apart to the use ordained by Christ, have such relation to him crucified, as that truly, although in terms used figuratively, they are sometimes called by the names of the things they represent, to wit, the \R{}body and blood of Christ, albeit, in substance and nature, they still remain truly and only \R{}bread and wine, as they were before.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{7}{1 Co 11:27}
\C{8}{1 Co 11:26,28}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{6}
That doctrine which maintains a change of the substance of bread and wine, into the substance of Christ’s body and blood, commonly called transubstantiation, by consecration of a priest, or by any other way, is repugnant not to Scripture \R{}alone, but even to common sense and reason, overthroweth the \R{}nature of the ordinance, and hath been, and is, the cause of manifold superstitions, yea, of gross idolatries.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{9}{Act 3:21; Luk 24:6,39}
\C{10}{1 Co 11:24-25}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{7}
Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible elements in this ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified, \R{}and all the benefits of his death; the body and blood of Christ being then not corporally or carnally, but spiritually present to the faith of believers in that ordinance, as the elements themselves are to their outward senses.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{11}{1 Co 10:16; 11:23-26}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{8}
All ignorant and ungodly persons, as they are unfit to enjoy communion \R{}with Christ, so are they unworthy of the Lord’s table, and cannot, without great sin against him, while they remain such, partake of these holy mysteries, \R{}or be admitted thereunto; yea, whosoever shall receive unworthily, are guilty of the body and blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgment to themselves.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{12}{2 Co 6:14-15}
\C{13}{1 Co 11:29; Mat 7:6}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Of the State of Man after Death and Of the Resurrection of the Dead}

\paragraph{1}
The bodies of men after death return to dust, \R{}and see corruption; but their souls, which neither die nor sleep, having an immortal subsistence, immediately \R{}return to God who gave them. The souls of the righteous being then made perfect in holiness, are received into paradise, where they are with Christ, and behold the face of God in light \R{}and glory, waiting for the full redemption of their bodies; and the souls of the wicked are cast into hell; where they remain in torment and utter darkness, reserved to \R{}the judgment of the great day; besides these two places, for souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Gen 3:19; Act 13:36}
\C{2}{Ecc 12:7}
\C{3}{Luk 23:43; 2 Co 5:1,6,8; Phi 1:23; Heb 12:3}
\C{4}{Jude 1:6-7; 1 Pe 3:19; Luk 16:23-24}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2}
At the last day, such of the saints as are found alive, shall not sleep, but be \R{}changed; and all the dead shall be raised up with the selfsame bodies, and \R{}none other; although with different \R{}qualities, which shall be united again to their souls forever.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{5}{1 Co 15:51-52; 1 Th 4:17}
\C{6}{Job 19:26-27}
\C{7}{1 Co 15:42-43}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3}

\chapter{Of the Last Judgment}

\paragraph{1}
God hath appointed a day wherein he will judge the world in righteousness, by \R{}Jesus Christ; to whom all power and judgment is given of the Father; in which day, not only  the \R{}apostate angels shall be judged, but likewise all persons that have lived upon the earth shall appear before the tribunal of Christ, \R{}to give an account of their thoughts, words, and deeds, and to receive according to what they have done in the body, whether good or evil.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{1}{Act 17:31; Joh 5:22,27}
\C{2}{1 Co 6:3; Jude 1:6}
\C{3}{2 Co 5:10; Ecc 12:14; Mat 12:36; Rom 14:10,12; Mat 25:32-46}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2} 
The end of God’s appointing this day, is for the manifestation of the glory of his mercy, in the eternal salvation of the elect; \R{}and of his justice, in the eternal damnation of the reprobate, who are wicked and disobedient; for then shall the righteous go into everlasting life, and receive that fulness of joy and glory with everlasting rewards, in the presence \R{}of the Lord; but the wicked, who know not God, and obey not the gospel of Jesus Christ, shall be cast aside into everlasting torments, and \R{}punished with everlasting destruction, from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power.\lb{}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\C{4}{Rom 9:22-23}
\C{5}{Mat 25:21,34; 2 Ti 4:8}
\C{6}{Mat 25:46; Mar 9:48; 2 Th 1:7-10}
\end{enumerate}
 
\paragraph{3} 
As Christ would have us to be certainly persuaded that there shall be a day of judgment, both \R{}to deter all men from sin, and for the greater \R{}consolation of the godly in their adversity, so will he have the day unknown to men, that they may shake off all carnal security, and be always watchful, because they know not at what hour the \R{}Lord will come, and may ever be prepared to say, \R{}Come Lord Jesus; come quickly. Amen.\lb{}
